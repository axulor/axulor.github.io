Research Interests

The throughline of my work is a single question: how does intelligent collective behavior arise from many simple, adaptive agents, in nature and in machines. Concretely, I work across three areas.

Collective intelligence
How robust, global behavior emerges from large populations of simple agents following local rules. I see this as evolutionary computation and open-ended adaptation in agent populations, and it is why nature-inspired AI interests me.
Game theory
I am drawn to the dynamic side of game theory more than its static equilibria.
  • Learning in games: how agents learn, adapt, and converge, or fail to, through repeated interaction.
  • Social dilemmas: how cooperation can survive when individual and collective incentives pull apart, a question at the heart of cooperative AI.
  • Equilibrium computation: solving games, especially imperfect-information games, through counterfactual regret minimization, and the computational complexity of finding equilibria (the PPAD versus P question).
Multi-agent systems & RL
The bridge from game-theoretic solution concepts to scalable learning algorithms: multi-agent reinforcement learning, and the deep methods that let these ideas run at scale.

Selected Research

2026 –
ongoing

Deep counterfactual regret minimization for imperfect-information games

An ongoing collaborative project where I proposed the core idea and mathematical formulation: a model-free deep CFR method with last-iterate convergence theory, on custom engines for Kuhn poker, Leduc poker, and Liar's Dice. Implemented and tested in PyTorch, with early experiments reproducing the predicted dynamics across tabular and sampling regimes.

Code: github.com/axulor/ACFR

2024 – 2026

Regret-driven learning and the emergence of cooperation on networks

Large-scale agent-based studies of how counterfactual reasoning, regret, and network structure shape whether cooperation arises and persists in spatial social-dilemma games. Built high-performance Monte-Carlo simulators in Python and C++. Outcomes: a first-author paper in Chaos and a manuscript in preparation for PNAS.

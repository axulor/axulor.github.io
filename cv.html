Research Summary

I study how cooperation, coordination, and structure emerge in large populations of simple, adaptive agents, using evolutionary game theory, learning dynamics, and large-scale agent-based simulation, a form of evolutionary computation and open-ended adaptation in agent populations. My recent work moves from regret-based learning in spatial social dilemmas toward deep multi-agent learning and the solving of imperfect-information games.

Selected Research

2026 –
ongoing

Deep counterfactual regret minimization for imperfect-information games

An ongoing collaborative project on model-free deep CFR with last-iterate convergence theory, built on custom game engines for Kuhn poker, Leduc poker, and Liar's Dice. Implemented in PyTorch, with early experiments reproducing the predicted dynamics across tabular and sampling regimes. Targeting a top machine-learning venue.

2024 – 2026

Regret-driven learning and the emergence of cooperation on networks

Large-scale agent-based studies of how counterfactual reasoning, regret, and network structure shape whether cooperation arises and persists in spatial social-dilemma games. Built high-performance Monte-Carlo simulators in Python and C++. Outcomes: a first-author paper in Chaos and a manuscript in preparation for PNAS.

Reproducible code at github.com/axulor/CARM.
